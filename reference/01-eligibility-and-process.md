# §58c StbG — Eligibility & the Filing Process

Austrian Citizenship Act (Staatsbürgerschaftsgesetz / StbG), **§58c** — the route for **descendants of people persecuted by the Nazi regime** to acquire Austrian (and therefore EU) citizenship.

Source-tier tags used inline: **[T1]** = official Austrian govt (BMEIA / oesterreich.gv.at / a consulate) · **[T2]** = reputable Austrian law firm · **[T3]** = blog/forum/press anecdote. FACTS are sourced; INFERENCE is labeled.

> **⚠️ Verify against official sources.** The authoritative references are **oesterreich.gv.at** (Austrian government service portal) and **bmeia.gv.at** (Foreign Ministry). Critically, **each Austrian embassy/consulate publishes its own "Citizenship for persecuted persons and their direct descendants" page** carrying the office-specific questionnaire link and document list — find *your responsible post's* page and treat it as controlling over any general summary, including this file. Laws and office practice drift; re-confirm load-bearing details before relying on them.

## 1. Eligibility

### The persecuted ancestor (the "anchor") must have — [T1]

Per the official BMEIA declaration pages, the anchor ancestor must have:

1. Held **Austrian citizenship**, a successor-state nationality of the former Austro-Hungarian Monarchy, **or been stateless**; AND
2. Had their **main residence in Austria**; AND
3. **Went abroad — emigrated from Austria — before 15 May 1955 because they suffered, or had reason to fear, persecution** by organs of the NSDAP / the German Reich — or because they defended the democratic Republic of Austria.

**The 15 May 1955 date bounds the DEPARTURE, not merely the residence.** [T1] The official wording is that the person "went abroad … before May 15, 1955." So the emigration itself must predate 15 May 1955 — an ancestor who remained in Austria until, say, 1957 does **not** satisfy this limb, even if they had a qualifying earlier residence and later feared persecution. (The related sub-limbs the statute lists — e.g. a person who *"did not have their main place of residence in Austria between 30 January 1933 and 9 May 1945 because they would have feared persecution,"* and persons who *"died due to persecution or were deported abroad before 9 May 1945"* — are variants of the same flight-because-of-persecution logic.)

**The persecuted person themselves may also declare**, not only descendants. [T1 — the London embassy page: *"If you are a persecuted person yourself, we would ask you to directly contact your local Austrian diplomatic representation."*] This is rarely relevant this many decades on, but it completes the picture.

Source [T1]: BMEIA §58c declaration pages (each consulate publishes its own — e.g. the Washington and London embassy "Citizenship for persecuted persons and their direct descendants" pages).

**A common qualifying pattern — the Kindertransport child.** A child who resided in Vienna (or elsewhere in Austria) and left on a Kindertransport in 1938/39 because of Nazi persecution fits limb 3 squarely. The departure is well before the 15 May 1955 departure cutoff. In cases like this the eligibility is usually clean on its face; **the live problem is almost always *documentary proof*, not eligibility in principle.**

### Which descendants qualify — [T1]

"**All direct descendants of a persecuted ancestor — children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren…**" including children adopted as minors. **There is no generational cap.**

- A grandchild of the persecuted ancestor qualifies. ✅
- A great-grandchild qualifies. ✅
- **The maternal line is fine.** The statute is "direct descending line," gender-neutral. Descent through a mother, or grandmother, carries no penalty. [T1]

### Key carve-outs — [T1]

- **Dual / multiple citizenship is allowed.** §58c is an explicit exception to Austria's normal renounce-your-other-citizenship rule. You keep the citizenships you already hold and add Austrian (EU). [T1 / T2]
- **No residence in Austria is required** to file or to hold the citizenship. [T2]
- **Each person files individually.** One family member becoming a citizen does NOT automatically confer it on others; each person needs their own declaration with the documented family chain. Minor children are filed *by a parent on the child's behalf* (a separate declaration per child). [T1 — minor-filing mechanics are only lightly documented officially; confirm the exact minor procedure with your competent consulate.]
- Applies to **Jews and non-Jews** — the persecution grounds are broad. (But see the gotchas file: non-Jewish / political-persecution claims are, in practice, harder to get approved.) [T2]
- **§58c is descent-only — spouses acquire NOTHING through it.** A husband or wife of a §58c beneficiary does **not** gain Austrian citizenship by the marriage. Only blood descendants (and minor-adopted descendants) of the persecuted ancestor qualify. [T1 / INFERENCE from the "direct descendant" framing]
- **Children born AFTER your acquisition date acquire Austrian citizenship automatically, by descent — no §58c declaration needed.** Only descendants who **already exist** on your acquisition date must file their own §58c declarations; a child born to you afterward inherits citizenship the ordinary way, as any child of an Austrian citizen (register the birth per normal Austrian citizenship-by-descent procedure with your consulate). [T1 — general Austrian nationality law; confirm registration mechanics with your post]

### Recent changes / current status — [T1 / T2]

- The descendant expansion **took effect 1 September 2020** — this is the version that already covered stateless persons and former Austro-Hungarian **successor-state** nationals (those weren't new in 2022).
- A **further amendment came into force 1 May 2022** (BGBl. I 48/2022), broadening eligibility in some edge cases. Per RIHS (T2) and the BMEIA pages (T1), the headline change: descendants of persecuted persons who **were Austrian citizens but did *not* have a main residence in the current Austrian federal territory** before persecution can now also claim — closing a hardship gap that had excluded people from the historical Monarchy periphery (e.g. Bohemia/Moravia, parts of present-day Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, western Ukraine). If your ancestor's situation is an edge case (citizenship affected via marriage, no main residence in today's Austria, etc.), **check the current official sources** — the precise contours of the 2022 broadening are best read off oesterreich.gv.at / bmeia.gv.at rather than any summary. [T2 / T1]
- **Still open as of 2026.** No sunset date or filing deadline has surfaced. §58c is framed as a permanent statutory entitlement (historical-responsibility reparation), not a time-limited program. Treat "no deadline" as *unconfirmed — no sunset found* rather than a firm guarantee, but there is no known pressure to rush on eligibility grounds.

## 2. The nature of the filing

- **It is a DECLARATION ("Anzeige"), not an application.** [T1] You *notify* the authority; you are not petitioning at its discretion. This is why a lawyer is legally optional.
- **A lawyer is NOT required.** Official wording [T1]: *"You do not need to engage a lawyer in order to file your declaration."* (Many applicants still hire a firm for full-service archive research, translations, apostilles, and authority liaison — a cost/convenience choice, not a legal necessity.)
- **The retroactive citizenship-acquisition date is the date MA35 *receives* the declaration** — not the date of approval. [T1] The statute keys on receipt by the **competent authority (MA35 in Vienna)**. Because the consulate is a forwarding mailbox, the receipt date can be **somewhat later than the day you physically hand your papers in** at the consulate — the two are not necessarily the same day. Filing sooner still locks in an earlier acquisition date, but confirm with your consulate **which date controls** in your case (submission-to-consulate vs. receipt-by-MA35). This matches the gotchas file, which quotes an applicant: *"everything is frozen at the date MA35 receives it; citizenship is backdated to that date."* (See the gotchas file for the tension between filing early vs. filing with a complete evidence set.)

### Post-approval sequence (what happens after MA35 says yes) [T1 / INFERENCE]

Once MA35 approves the declaration, the citizenship documents issue in a sequence — you don't get a passport straight away:

1. **Decision (*Bescheid*)** — MA35 issues the formal decision confirming the acquisition (you typically sign/return a document it sends).
2. **Citizenship certificate (*Staatsbürgerschaftsnachweis*)** — the official proof-of-citizenship document.
3. **Only then** can you apply for an **Austrian passport / national ID card** (at your consulate), which is the usable EU travel document most applicants are actually after. (The passport stage may ask for civil-status documents — e.g. a marriage certificate — that the *declaration* itself didn't require.)

## 3. Where to file — jurisdiction by residence

- **You file at the Austrian embassy / consulate whose consular district covers your MAIN RESIDENCE.** [T1] This is **exclusive** — verbatim official wording: *"the Austrian diplomatic representation in the consular district where you have your main residence is responsible for your case … no other local representation can support you in submitting your declaration nor accept it."*
- **This has sharp consequences for families filing together.** If family members have their main residence in different consular districts, they **cannot all file at one office**. Each files at their own district's post. Adult children away at college may be in a different district than the parent — pin down whether "main residence" is the college address or the permanent home address.
- **Consular posts differ in how they handle §58c — verify yours.** Some applicants have reported a particular post routing their §58c declaration to a *different* office rather than processing it locally. Practice appears to change over time and by post, so treat any specific "post X doesn't do §58c" claim as needing re-confirmation. **Check whether YOUR responsible consulate processes §58c** — via that post's own "Citizenship for persecuted persons and their direct descendants" page and the official consular-jurisdiction list (published by the embassies, e.g. austria.org's consular-jurisdiction PDF) — and map your address to the correct post; don't assume the nearest city. [T3 — routing anecdotes; confirm against T1 for your post]
- **The consulate is a mailbox, not the decider.** The consular post forwards the declaration + documents to **MA35 (Vienna Municipal Department 35)**, the competent deciding authority. The consulate does no pre-evaluation and cannot tell you whether you'll win.

### Coordination lever for families — the ancestor packet is submitted only ONCE [T1]

When a family files collectively (even across different consulates), **the documents about the persecuted ancestor need to be submitted only once** — the others can reference the previously filed declarations by including a brief note with their own submission. Practically:

- The first filer submits the full ancestor evidence packet.
- The others file at their own consulate and attach a **cover note** referencing the prior filing (applicant names, DOBs, which consulate/date each was filed at, and that all descend from the same recognized persecuted ancestor).
- (Because MA35 searches sloppily — see the gotchas file — a robust approach is to make **each** file self-contained with a full copy of the ancestor chain anyway, plus the cross-reference note. Confirm the exact cross-reference mechanics with each consulate.)

## 4. Fees & timeline

- **The declaration itself is FREE.** [T1] Verbatim: *"The granting of Austrian citizenship is free of charge."* Federal fees are exempted by law; if you were **not born in Austria and/or do not live in Austria**, the Province of Vienna is responsible and charges **no provincial fees** either. You pay only for apostilles, sworn translations, and any records you order yourself.
- **Timeline: no official figure is published.** [T1] Law-firm estimates cluster around **3–9 months** [T2], but lived experience runs longer and is highly variable — plan for **12–24 months**, hope for the fast end. (Detailed timeline data + why the official figure is optimistic is in the gotchas file.)

## 5. The online questionnaire (do this first)

- **Complete the BMEIA online §58c questionnaire for your competent office first.** Each consulate has its own office-specific link (the BMEIA `info58c` questionnaire, parameterized by office). It's offered in several languages (typically German / English / Spanish / Hebrew).
- The questionnaire **generates an individualized document list AND a pre-filled declaration form** ("Anzeigeformular") to print, sign, and submit.
- A started session is typically valid for a limited window (~72 hours) — set aside time to finish it once you begin.
- **Each adult files their own questionnaire + own declaration.** The adult form does not fold in adult children — an adult child is a separate declarant (relationship to anchor = e.g. great-grandchild). A parent runs a **minor-supplement** only for minor children.
- **Answer the "fled persecution" limb carefully.** The form asks, in more than one place, whether the ancestor had a main residence in Austria and whether they went abroad because of feared/suffered persecution. For a textbook "fled" case: the ancestor **did** have an Austrian residence until they fled (so "no main residence in Austria" answers are **No**), and the "went abroad because of persecution" answer is **Yes** — that Yes is the qualifying basis and must stay Yes. Read each item twice; a mis-click here misstates your qualifying limb.

## 6. Per-person document checklist [T1]

Distinguish three document groups — they have **different format requirements**:

### A. Documents about YOUR OWN person (strictest)

- **Valid passport(s)** — proving each citizenship you hold (returned after copying).
- **Your own birth certificate** — a **fresh certified original**, then **apostilled** by the issuing authority (for a US applicant: the issuing **state's Secretary of State**).
- **A recent criminal-record extract** (less than the validity window old), issued at the **national/federal** level of your country of residence. *Example — US residents:* the **FBI Identity History Summary** (fingerprint-based, via the FBI or an approved Channeler); a **state**-level criminal-record certificate is **not** sufficient. Residents of other countries submit their own country's equivalent national police / criminal-record certificate (e.g. a UK ACRO Police Certificate, a German *Führungszeugnis*, etc.). (Where that document gets apostilled/legalised is discussed in the gotchas file.)
- **EU-size passport photo** (typically 3.5×4.5 to 4.0×5.0 cm), if requested.
- Marriage / divorce certificates only if a **surname changed through marriage/divorce in your own line** — otherwise usually not needed for the declaration (may be needed later at the *passport* stage).

### B. Descent-chain documents (ancestor → you)

Each link in the chain, imaged:

- Your parent's / grandparent's birth certificate (the one that names the persecuted ancestor, ideally by **maiden name**).
- Marriage certificates for any name change **in the direct line**.
- The persecuted ancestor's birth record (often a GAP — see below).
- Death certificates where applicable.

**Format relief [T1]:** documents about the **persecuted ancestor and about the intermediate ancestors** typically do **not** require an apostille and **may be submitted as plain photocopies**. Only your *own-person* documents (§A) need apostilles. (Confirm with your consulate — one consular reply stated exactly this: only the applicant's own birth cert + own criminal-record certificate need apostilles; ancestor docs and the passport are plain photocopies.)

⚠️ **Format ≠ possession.** "No apostille / photocopy is fine" changes the *format*, not whether you must still *have* the document. You still need to obtain each descent-chain record — you just don't have to certify/apostille it.

### C. Documents about the PERSECUTED ANCESTOR (all "if available")

- Austrian residence proof (historical registration record — a *Meldezettel* / *Meldekarte*).
- Departure / flight proof (e.g. Kindertransport or passenger records, ~1938/39).
- Persecution proof (refugee classification, victim recognition, etc.).

**Big relief [T1]:** *"In case you do not have any documents concerning your persecuted ancestor, you do not have to ask for documentation to be searched and sent from Austria. The Austrian authorities will try to find evidence in archives."* The competent provincial authority *"will research the necessary documents … about your direct ancestors in various archives as far as possible."* So the hardest ancestor-side gaps (birth record, last Austrian address) are **largely Austria's job** — you may file **without** the ancestor's Austrian birth record; MA35 checks its own databases. (But see the gotchas file: MA35's self-research is unreliable for complex cases, so front-loading standard registry documents still helps.)

### D. Authentication & translation [T1]

- Documents **not in German or English** need a certified translation by a **sworn, judicially certified translator**.
- **English-language documents do NOT need translation** unless MA35 specifically asks — a major simplification for records originating in English-speaking countries.
- Apostille only where §A requires it; ancestor/descent docs generally do not.

## 7. Own-person document mechanics (the practical bottleneck)

Wherever you live, the perishable, effort-heavy items are the **two own-person documents that need authentication**: your birth certificate and your criminal-record certificate. The *shape* is the same everywhere; the specific offices differ by country of residence.

**General shape (any country):**

1. **Own birth certificate → apostille (or legalisation).** A certificate you already hold is often only "half good." Order a **fresh certified original**; then have it **apostilled under the Hague Apostille Convention by your country's competent authority** (the body that issues apostilles for civil-registry documents in your country). If your country is **not** party to the Hague Convention, you instead need **consular legalisation** rather than an apostille — confirm the chain with the Austrian consulate.

2. **National criminal-record certificate → apostille/legalisation by the competent authority for that document type.** Note that the birth cert and the criminal-record cert may be authenticated by **different offices** in the same country (e.g. a regional authority for civil records vs. a national/foreign-ministry authority for federal documents) — don't assume one office handles both.

3. **Certified/sworn German translation** for any document **not in German or English**. Documents already in **German or English** need no translation.

**Example — US-resident applicant:**

1. **Own birth certificate → *state* apostille.** Order a **fresh certified long-form original**, stating it is "for international purposes" (this often generates an **exemplification letter**); the county clerk certifies/notarizes the signature as needed; then the **issuing state's Secretary of State issues the apostille.** The apostille comes from the **state that issued the certificate** — if you and your children were born in different states, each birth cert routes through a different state's Secretary of State.

2. **FBI Identity History Summary → *federal* apostille.** The FBI report is a **federal** document, so its apostille comes from the **US Department of State Office of Authentications (Washington DC)**, **NOT** a state Secretary of State — a concrete case of the "different offices" point above. (Whether the FBI report needs an apostille *at all* is genuinely disputed among applicants — ask your consular handler before paying for it; see the gotchas file.)

3. **Sworn translations** — only for non-English documents; US/UK English-language records need none.

**Sequencing warning:** the FBI report has a short validity window (commonly cited as ~6 months) measured against your **submission** date. Don't pull it too early while waiting on the birth-cert/apostille track, or it expires before you file and you re-pull. Once the file reaches MA35 within the window, it's frozen and won't "expire" mid-processing.
