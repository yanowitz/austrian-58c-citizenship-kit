# §58c Evidence — Source & Archive Playbook

A field guide to the archives and databases that hold proof of a Nazi-persecuted
Austrian ancestor and the descent chain, for a §58c StbG citizenship declaration.
For each source: what it holds, why it matters, how to reach it, whether it's
web-searchable or request-only, how to search it (name-variant tips included),
turnaround, and cost.

> **Fees below are current as of mid-2026 and drift — check each provider's live
> page before relying on a figure.**

**IMPORTANT — Austria does much of this for you.** Under §58c the competent
Austrian authority (Vienna's Municipal Dept. 35 / MA 35) will research its own
archives for the persecuted ancestor's Austrian birth, citizenship, and residence
records "as far as possible." Official BMEIA wording: *"In case you do not have any
documents concerning your persecuted ancestor, you do not have to ask for
documentation to be searched and sent from Austria. The Austrian authorities will
try to find evidence in archives."* So the sources below are mostly about (a)
building your own persuasive evidence bundle, and (b) filling gaps you'd rather not
leave to the authority. You can file the declaration *before* the ancestor's
Austrian records are in hand.

---

## Name-variant discipline (read first)

Austrian/German names get mangled crossing borders and decades. Before searching
ANY database, list every plausible spelling of the ancestor's surname and given
name and search each one:

- **Umlauts:** `ü` is written `ue` in ASCII contexts and `u` when the mark is
  dropped — so one name yields three strings (e.g. a surname with `ü` becomes the
  `ü`, `ue`, and `u` spellings).
  Many databases normalize umlauts internally, but not all; supply all forms.
- **Given-name drift:** a middle name may appear as `Isidor` / `Isador` / `Isidore`
  across German vs. English records. Treat **surname + date of birth** as the
  primary match keys and don't let a middle-name spelling difference exclude an
  otherwise-matching record — say this explicitly in written requests.
- **Married vs. maiden name:** a woman who fled single and married later appears in
  arrival/registration records under her **maiden** name and in later records under
  her **married** name. Search both, and tell each archive which name applies to
  which period.
- **Anschluss nationality:** after March 1938 Austria was annexed, so UK/Allied
  wartime records often list an Austrian-born refugee's nationality as **"German."**
  This is expected, not a discrepancy — the §58c authority handles it routinely.
- Turn on **phonetic / fuzzy / Soundex** matching where a database offers it.

## How this playbook is organized — two tiers

- **Tier A — Core / destination-independent sources.** Austrian-side records and
  international Holocaust-victim databases that **every** applicant uses, no matter
  where their ancestor ended up. These prove Austrian origin, Austrian residence,
  and persecution. Start here.
- **Tier B — Destination-country sources.** Records that exist only because the
  ancestor **resettled somewhere** after fleeing. The persecuted ancestors in these
  cases went to many countries — the **UK, the USA, Palestine/Israel, France,
  Shanghai, Argentina, Australia**, and elsewhere. This playbook works the **UK** set
  in full concrete detail as the example, then gives a shorter "if your ancestor fled
  elsewhere" guide to the equivalent record types per destination. **Substitute your
  own ancestor's destination country.**

## Which to hit first (ordering)

1. **Free, self-service, instant — do these first (same day):**
   Arolsen Archives online collections · DÖW victim databases · Yad Vashem Names
   database · USHMM Holocaust Survivors and Victims DB · your destination country's
   online immigration/naturalisation catalogue (for the UK: TNA *Discovery*). These
   cost nothing and tell you within minutes whether a record exists.
2. **Free request-only (file early — slow clocks):**
   Your destination country's refugee-aid-organisation records (UK: World Jewish
   Relief / CBF) · Arolsen full-file inquiry (for documents withheld online) · any
   closed-file opening request (UK: TNA FOI for a closed HO 405 file). These are free
   but can take weeks to many months, so start them early.
3. **Paid request-only (file when you need the specific record):**
   Wiener Stadt- und Landesarchiv Meldeanfrage (last Vienna address) · IKG Wien
   Matriken/archive (Jewish vital records) · Austrian State Archives ÖStA
   (Aryanization/asset files) · destination-country civil-registration certificates
   (UK: GRO) · destination-country census/registration (UK: 1939 Register via
   Findmypast, subscription). Order these once you've confirmed via the finding aids
   that the record exists and is worth the fee.
4. **Let the Austrian authority pull it:** the ancestor's **Vienna birth record**
   and, often, the **last Vienna residence** — MA 35 researches these itself, and
   an official request from a consulate or ministry is fee-exempt at IKG/WStLA.

# TIER A — CORE / DESTINATION-INDEPENDENT SOURCES

These are the records everyone uses regardless of where the ancestor fled. They
establish Austrian origin, Austrian residence, and persecution.

---

## Arolsen Archives (International Tracing Service / ITS)

- **What it holds:** the world's largest archive on Nazi persecution — tracing and
  documentation (T/D) case files, concentration-camp and displaced-persons records,
  postwar tracing correspondence. Case files opened by relatives searching for
  survivors after the war are common and directly document a person as
  persecuted/displaced.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** a T/D case in the ancestor's name is independent,
  third-party corroboration that they were persecuted and displaced — squarely
  on-point for the "left because of persecution" element. Postwar tracing letters
  can also pin the ancestor's location, occupation, and family links.
- **Access URL:** online collections at
  https://collections.arolsen-archives.org — searchable free.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **both.** Case metadata and many scans are
  viewable free online after accepting a sensitive-data consent modal. Some
  documents are withheld online for data protection; unlock the full file via a
  **free inquiry** at https://arolsen-archives.org/en/search-explore/inquiries/
  (guided multi-step form). Cite the exact case/reference number so it routes to
  the file.
- **How to search:** search the surname (all variants) and filter by date of birth.
  A "Topic/case" hit gives a reference code like `0xxxxxxx.x.xxx.xxx`. Ignore
  near-miss People-index entries with the wrong DOB.
- **Turnaround:** online = instant. Full-file inquiry = **slow: average ~5 months,
  up to ~12 months.** File it early.
- **Cost:** free.

## DÖW — Documentation Centre of Austrian Resistance (Gedenkbuch)

- **What it holds:** databases of Austrian victims of National Socialism —
  Holocaust victims (the murdered), Gestapo victims, politically persecuted, and
  the Spiegelgrund child-euthanasia dead. Includes **official deportation records**
  (transport, date, destination, prisoner number, date of death).
- **Why it matters for §58c:** if the ancestor's **parents or relatives were
  deported and murdered**, DÖW's deportation record is among the strongest possible
  persecution evidence and adds enormous weight to the family's case. Note the
  logic: an ancestor who **survived by fleeing will be ABSENT** from a
  murdered-victims register — that absence is the normal signature of a successful
  escape, **not** evidence against the claim. The affirmative proof of a survivor's
  flight lives in the refugee/tracing archives instead.
- **Access URL:** https://www.doew.at/personensuche — public, self-service.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable, free.**
- **How to search:** enter the surname with **phonetic search ON**; it searches all
  the victim databases at once. Check every near-match's DOB and birthplace.
- **Turnaround:** instant.
- **Cost:** free.

## Yad Vashem — Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names

- **What it holds:** names and biographical records of Holocaust victims, from
  Pages of Testimony (family-submitted memorial pages), deportation lists, and
  community records. Since 2021 it also includes **survivor** names, not only the
  murdered.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** Pages of Testimony can name the ancestor's parents,
  birthplace, and fate, and (crucially) reveal who submitted the page — a survivor
  submitting a page for murdered parents both documents the parents' fate and links
  the survivor to them. Strong persecution and family-chain evidence.
- **Access URL:** https://collections.yadvashem.org/en/names (also
  https://yvng.yadvashem.org).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable, free.**
- **How to search:** surname (all variants) + given name; use synonym/Soundex
  options. Records link to related entries (deportation lists, inmate lists). The
  names backend occasionally returns a `504` server timeout — that is an
  infrastructure outage, **not** "no record"; retry later.
- **Turnaround:** instant.
- **Cost:** free.

---

## USHMM — Holocaust Survivors and Victims Database

- **What it holds:** a large aggregation of survivor and victim records from many
  source collections; also hosts microfilmed community and organizational records.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** a broad cross-check; also the access point for some
  microfilmed IKG Vienna records (below).
- **Access URL:** https://www.ushmm.org/online/hsv (advanced person search).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable, free.** Fields: last/first/
  maiden name (exact/fuzzy/Soundex), birth year ±5/±10, birthplace, nationality.
- **How to search:** run fuzzy surname + given name, and surname + birthplace, as
  separate queries. As with the other victim DBs, absence of a survivor is expected.
- **Note:** the database has been migrating to a new URL — if a search page 404s,
  find the current location from ushmm.org.
- **Turnaround:** instant. **Cost:** free.

## IKG Wien — Jewish Community of Vienna (archive + Matrikenamt)

- **What it holds:** Jewish-community vital records (*Matriken* — births, marriages,
  deaths, historically 1784–1938), community/membership and tax files, emigration
  and deportation lists, and notably the lists of Vienna Kindertransport departures
  and a "List of Jews living in Vienna as of 15 September 1939." A **1920 Vienna
  Jewish birth record** is a Matriken entry held here.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** the ancestor's **Vienna Jewish birth record** proves
  Austrian origin and names the parents (a key search key for further records); the
  parents' synagogue marriage record anchors Jewish descent; Kindertransport lists
  and the 1939 residents list can document flight/departure.
- **Access URL/contact:** https://www.ikg-wien.at/en/archive · for a birth register
  (Matriken) write **matriken@ikg-wien.at** (Records Office); for emigration/
  deportation/burial holdings write **archiv@ikg-wien.at** (general Archive) — CC
  both. Postal: IKG Wien, Archiv/Matriken, Seitenstettengasse 4, 1010 Vienna.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **request-only** — no public online victim
  database. It's a formal, **fee-based** written process with a **required signed
  user form ("Data Sheet")**; scan and email it in. No walk-in or phone requests.
- **How to search / what to send:** completed & signed user declaration form, a copy
  of your photo ID, and proof of descent (a record naming the ancestor as your
  parent/grandparent). Give all surname variants + exact DOB. State purpose =
  "family research" (and/or "official purpose"). They pull a specific named record;
  they do **not** build family trees.
- **Fees:** written research **€50 per started half-hour**; extensive family research
  **€140 per half-hour** (they send a *Kostenvoranschlag* / cost estimate first);
  civil-status certificate **€12/A4**; scan of an original **€6/A4**; non-EU postage
  **€13**. **Fees are owed even if nothing is found.** No descendant discount.
- **FEE-EXEMPTION LEVER:** official requests from **embassies/consulates/ministries/
  courts are fee-exempt.** The cheapest path is to let the consulate or the Austrian
  authority pull the record as part of the §58c process, rather than paying the
  research fee for a parallel copy.
- **Access rule for old records:** a birth register carries a 100-year protective
  period; a 1920 entry cleared that in 2020. A direct descendant with "credible
  legal interest" may obtain a certificate on submitting ID + proof of descent.
- **Turnaround:** not published. **Language:** page/forms in English; whether they
  *reply* in English is not stated.

## GenTeam.at — free online index to Vienna Jewish (and Austrian) vital records

- **What it holds:** a large **free** genealogical database run by an Austrian
  volunteer association, including **indexes to Vienna Jewish community (IKG) birth,
  marriage, and death records (*Matriken*)**, Catholic and Protestant registers,
  Vienna cemetery and death records, and more. It is an **index/finding aid**, not
  the certificates themselves — but it often lets you pinpoint the **exact Matriken
  register, year, and entry number** for an ancestor.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** arguably the **#1 free first stop for Vienna Jewish
  vital records.** Finding the exact IKG register entry *here, for free* means you can
  then request the specific certificate from IKG (or let the consulate/authority pull
  it fee-exempt) instead of paying IKG to *search* for it — a real cost saver, and it
  gives you the precise citation to hand MA35.
- **Access URL:** **https://www.genteam.at** — free, but you must **register a free
  account** to search.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable index, free** (after free
  registration). The underlying certificate is then ordered from IKG / the holding
  archive.
- **How to search:** register, then search the surname (all variants — umlaut `ü` /
  `ue` / `u`) across the Jewish-records datasets; note the register/entry citation for
  any hit and carry it to IKG. Give given name + DOB to disambiguate common surnames.
- **Turnaround:** instant for the index search. **Cost:** free.

## JewishGen — Austria-Czech / Vienna databases and global Jewish genealogy

- **What it holds:** the largest free Jewish-genealogy portal (a Museum of Jewish
  Heritage affiliate). For §58c the key pieces are the **Austria-Czech Special
  Interest Group (SIG)** databases and **Vienna** datasets — indexed Jewish vital
  records, community lists, Holocaust and emigration data — plus the **JewishGen
  Unified Search** across hundreds of collections, and **JOWBR** (burial registry).
- **Why it matters for §58c:** a free **index/lookup layer** parallel to GenTeam —
  use both, since their coverage overlaps but isn't identical. A hit gives you names,
  dates, and often the source citation to chase the underlying record; the
  Austria-Czech SIG is specifically oriented to the former Habsburg lands your
  ancestor likely came from. Good for cross-checking parents' names and birthplaces
  before you pay any archive.
- **Access URL:** **https://www.jewishgen.org** — free registration to search most
  databases (a suggested donation supports it but isn't required to search).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable, free** (after free
  registration).
- **How to search:** register; run the **Unified Search** on each surname variant +
  given name, then drill into the Austria-Czech SIG and Vienna databases directly.
  Treat results as index pointers — verify against the primary record before relying
  on them in the file.
- **Turnaround:** instant. **Cost:** free.

## Wiener Stadt- und Landesarchiv (WStLA / MA 8) — Meldeanfrage

- **What it holds:** the historical residence-registration records (*Meldezettel*)
  of Vienna — the door for a person's **last registered Vienna address** and their
  **deregistration (Abmeldung)** entry. A Meldezettel captures name, DOB/place,
  marital status, profession, religion, old and new residence, and arrival/departure
  dates.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** an official archive letter stating the ancestor's
  **last Vienna address** and the deregistration/departure entry directly documents
  Austrian residence and flight — two elements the claim rests on. This is often the
  one document a family can't otherwise produce itself.
- **Coverage:** roughly **1904–1975**, *provided deregistration occurred by 1975.* A
  person who fled in 1938–39 was deregistered then — well within the window, so
  their record should be here (not at MA 62, which holds post-1975 registrations).
  The office split is by **deregistration date**, not simply by year.
- **Access URL/contact:** the access-restricted written channel is the key one —
  https://www.wien.gv.at/english/e-government/culture/archives/research/residence-registration-records.html
  (English page → online form on mein.wien.gv.at). Email **post@archiv.wien.gv.at**
  for case-relevant follow-up. Postal: Wiener Stadt- und Landesarchiv (MA 8),
  Guglgasse 14, Gasometer D, 1110 Vienna.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **request-only.** Because the data is
  access-restricted, it *"can only be requested in writing"* via the online form;
  the general web enquiry form is for other research. You receive a copy of the
  *data*, not the original document.
- **What to supply:** full first + family name, **plus at least one identifying
  feature** — DOB, profession, or a residential address at a point in time. For a
  common surname, birthplace/profession is essential. Supply **all surname
  variants**. Have descent proof ready in case they ask (a data-protected release to
  a non-subject may hinge on it). For a deceased ancestor, the "person deceased"
  route can avoid the proof-of-descent/legal-interest gate — state that they are
  your late relative.
- **Fees:** **€35 per commenced half-hour**, max **2 hours → €140 ceiling.** Charged
  **even if the search is unsuccessful.** Copies/digitalisation extra.
- **Turnaround:** **within 2 months.** Reply is issued **in German** (submit in
  English is fine; translate the reply on your end).
- **Holocaust-era gotcha:** some Jewish Meldezettel may have been removed during the
  Nazi period, so an empty result doesn't necessarily mean the person was
  unregistered — but an *emigrant* who deregistered in 1938–39 is more likely to
  have a surviving Abmeldung card than a deportee. If the clerk can't find them, ask
  them to confirm they searched the **pre-1975 Meldearchiv**, not just refer you to
  MA 62.

## Austrian State Archives — ÖStA (Aryanization / asset & restitution files)

- **What it holds:** among much else, the Nazi-era **property/asset declarations**
  (*Vermögensanmeldung*, filed by Jews in 1938 with the *Vermögensverkehrsstelle* /
  VVSt), seized-claims files (FLD), and postwar restitution records — held in the
  *Archiv der Republik* (AdR).
- **Why it matters for §58c:** a 1938 compulsory Jewish asset declaration is a
  **foundational primary Austrian-state persecution document** naming the ancestor,
  with DOB, profession, and address — direct evidence the ancestor was persecuted as
  a Jew, and often the strongest state-issued proof against a *direct-line* ancestor.
- **Access URL/contact:** ordered from ÖStA — **gdpost@oesta.gv.at**,
  +43 1 795 40 0. Files are identified by an archival signature like
  `AT-OeStA/AdR/E-uReang/VVSt/VA/[file no.]`.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** the **finding aid** is searchable (see
  findbuch.at, below) — use it to identify the exact file and signature — then the
  physical file is **ordered by written request** (they send a *Kostenvoranschlag* /
  cost estimate first).
- **How to search:** find the signature via findbuch.at, then email ÖStA the
  signature and request a copy/estimate.
- **Turnaround:** varies (estimate first, then fulfilment). **Cost:** by estimate.

## findbuch.at — Findbuch for Victims of National Socialism (finding aid)

- **What it holds:** an online finding aid / index (200,000+ records) across
  Austrian NS property-seizure, restitution, and compensation holdings — VA
  (Vermögensanmeldung), FLD (seized claims), VEAV (notification of seized property),
  RK (restitution commission), SSt, Commercial Register, and Compensation-Fund
  datasets, spanning multiple Austrian archives (ÖStA and WStLA).
- **Why it matters for §58c:** it's the **discovery layer** — search it to find
  whether a persecution/asset file exists for the ancestor (or their parents), get
  the **archival signature**, and see which archive holds it (ÖStA vs. WStLA), so you
  can then order the physical file. It surfaces primary Austrian-state persecution
  records on the direct-line ancestor.
- **Access URL:** https://www.findbuch.at (register for a free login to search).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **web-searchable index** (free, after
  registration); the underlying **files are ordered** from the holding archive.
  Note the index tells you the record exists; some physical files are marked "no
  file available" (destroyed; index row only) and some hold only metadata.
- **How to search:** search the surname (umlauts are normalized — the `ü`, `ue`, and
  `u` spellings return the same set) and sort by name to sweep a cluster. **Caveats:**
  (a) it does **NOT** index historical address directories (Lehmann's Adressbuch) —
  an address string only matches address fields *within* records; for
  address-directory corroboration use digital.wienbibliothek.at / ANNO instead;
  (b) a shared file number can be a **multi-family bundle** (claims grouped by
  third-party debtor), so co-listing under one file number is **not** proof of
  kinship — verify with DOB/address before inferring family links;
  (c) a **murdered** relative may have filed nothing and be absent, while a
  **survivor** may have a **postwar restitution** entry.
- **Turnaround:** instant for the search. **Cost:** free to search; the ordered file
  costs per the holding archive.

## National Fund of the Republic of Austria (victim recognition)

- **What it holds:** records of gesture payments / recognition of victim status for
  victims of National Socialism. No public online lookup exists.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** a victim-recognition record is a strong evidence
  class in approved cases; even for a deceased ancestor a file record may exist. This
  is an **Austrian-side** source, so it applies regardless of where the ancestor fled.
- **Access URL/contact:** written enquiry only — **office@nationalfonds.org**;
  see **nationalfonds.org** for the current postal address and phone (the Fund's
  seat is tied to the Austrian Parliament and its address has shifted during
  Parliament renovations, so use the website's current contact block rather than a
  hard-coded street address).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **request-only** (no public database).
- **How to search:** email the ancestor's details (all name variants, DOB, fate) and
  ask whether any record exists; be ready to supply proof of descent. Note gesture
  payments were for *living* victims, so a payment record is less likely for a
  deceased ancestor — but a file/registration may still exist.
- **Turnaround:** varies. **Cost:** free enquiry.

# TIER B — DESTINATION-COUNTRY SOURCES

Records that exist because the ancestor **resettled** somewhere after fleeing Austria.
The fully-worked example below is the **UK**. If your ancestor fled elsewhere, read
the UK set for the *pattern* — immigration/naturalisation file, a refugee-aid
organisation's records, a national census/registration, and civil-registration
certificates — then jump to the **"If your ancestor fled elsewhere"** section for the
equivalent sources per country. **Substitute your ancestor's destination throughout.**

---

## UK National Archives (Kew) — Discovery, HO 396, HO 405, naturalisation

*(Destination example: ancestor fled to Britain.)*
The single richest UK source for an Austrian refugee who fled to Britain.
**Discovery** is the free online catalogue: https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk

- **HO 396 — Aliens Department Internees Index ("enemy alien" tribunal cards).**
  - *What/why:* the 1939–40 wartime tribunal card for each "enemy alien." For a
    genuine refugee it is typically headed **"Exemption from Internment — Refugee"**
    — an **official UK government document classifying the ancestor a refugee**, with
    exact identity, birthplace, DOB, address, and the tribunal decision/date. This is
    often the **load-bearing persecution/flight evidence**: it independently proves
    the person was in the UK by 1939 as a recognized refugee.
  - *Access:* Discovery catalogue (free); the card image is often **already digitised
    and searchable online via Ancestry** (the Wiener Library HO 396 project), so a
    scan may be obtainable without a Kew order. Otherwise order a copy from Kew.
  - *Note:* Austrian-born refugees are usually listed nationality **"German"** here
    (post-Anschluss) — expected. A refugee from a non-enemy country (e.g. Romania)
    won't have an HO 396 card; that absence is expected, not a gap.
- **Naturalisation certificates — series HO 334.**
  - *What/why:* the certificate of British naturalisation. Note historical practice:
    a wife was often naturalised **on her husband's certificate**, so there may be
    **no standalone certificate in her name** — she appears as "wife's name …" on
    his. The certificate pins the exact date and certificate number.
  - *Access:* search Discovery under all name variants; order an open certificate
    copy from Kew (see cost below).
- **HO 405 — Aliens' personal case files (applications for naturalisation).**
  - *What/why:* the deep file — police reports, the application, and refugee
    background. A wife's supporting papers (her 1938–39 arrival, refugee history)
    often sit **inside her husband's HO 405 file**. The single richest genealogical/
    persecution source when it survives.
  - *Access / status:* held at Kew but under **100-year closure**; roughly 40% survive
    (the rest destroyed). To open a closed-but-catalogued HO 405 file, submit **The
    National Archives' Freedom of Information request** against the file's reference —
    you FOI *TNA* for the catalogued file, not the Home Office directly. On a closed
    record's Discovery page, use the **"Submit FOI request"** button (pre-tags the
    form to that reference). Free. Response due in ~20 working days (+10 if TNA
    consults the Home Office). (Template in `03-email-request-templates.md`.)
- **How to search Discovery:** the catalogue detail pages are JS-rendered; if a
  plain fetch returns empty, use the Discovery **JSON API**
  (`discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/API/search/records`) or the site search UI.
  Run every surname variant × each series. Spelling in the catalogue may differ from
  yours (e.g. `Isador`/`Isidore`) — search loosely.
- **Cost / turnaround (record copies):** a certified copy is roughly **£25–26 +
  postage**, up to ~16 working days (standard/uncertified copies cheaper). Confirm
  on the live order page — prices drift.

## Findmypast — 1939 Register + GRO indexes

- **What it holds:** the **1939 Register** (a national register of England & Wales
  taken 29 Sept 1939 — name, exact DOB, occupation, address) and indexes to GRO
  civil registration (births, marriages, deaths).
- **Why it matters for §58c:** the 1939 Register is a **second official government
  record placing the refugee in the UK by late September 1939** — corroborating
  flight timing and giving occupation/address. Its marriage-index entries give the
  GRO District/Volume/Page needed to order a certificate.
- **Access URL:** findmypast.co.uk — **subscription/trial** required to view images.
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** searchable behind a paywall.
- **How to search:** search surname variants + given name + birth year; the register
  reference looks like `RG101/xxxxx/xxx/xx`. **Tip:** download full-resolution images
  while logged in — the download link only works during an active session, so grab
  images before a trial lapses.
- **Turnaround:** instant. **Cost:** subscription/free-trial.

## GRO — UK General Register Office (civil-registration certificates)

- **What it holds:** official England & Wales birth, marriage, and death
  certificates.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** these are the **descent-chain** documents — a
  UK-born parent's birth certificate (naming the ancestor by maiden name), the
  ancestor's UK marriage certificate (documenting a maiden→married surname change),
  death certificates where relevant. FreeBMD (free) is the index; GRO issues the
  certificate.
- **Access URL:** order online at the GRO ordering service (gov.uk); index-search
  free at https://www.freebmd.org.uk (England & Wales only).
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** index searchable free; the **certificate is
  ordered** (you need District + Volume + Page, or the GRO index reference).
- **How to search:** find the District/Volume/Page on FreeBMD or Findmypast, then
  order. FreeBMD blocks automated reads — read the reference off the page yourself.
- **Cost / turnaround:** roughly **£12.50** for a standard certificate (the GRO
  standard fee as of mid-2026 — it has risen over time, so check the live GRO page);
  a **priority** certificate with courier is more (order-time option). Standard
  dispatch in a few working days; priority faster. For §58c a plain photocopy
  suffices — no apostille needed on ancestor/descent-chain docs (see below).

## World Jewish Relief / Central British Fund (CBF) — refugee records

- **What it holds:** the archive of the Central British Fund and the Refugee
  Children's Movement and successors — registration slips for ~277,000 people and
  case files for ~35,000 refugees, explicitly covering Austria/Germany refugees and
  Kindertransport arrivals. Records include a registration card (name, nationality,
  birthplace, DOB, profession, addresses) and, where one survives, a fuller case
  file.
- **Why it matters for §58c:** an independent refugee-registry record directly
  documents the ancestor as a Jewish refugee rescued from Nazi Europe — strong
  "fled persecution" evidence. A card marked with a **"Movement number"** indicates
  an official **Kindertransport** arrival; a card lacking it suggests another route
  (e.g. an older teenager arriving as a nurse or trainee). A case file can name
  parents (guarantors), addresses, and siblings.
- **Access URL:** the "Find Your Family Story" / archives request form at
  https://www.worldjewishrelief.org/what-we-do/archives-your-family-history/
- **Web-searchable or request-only:** **request-only** (no public search); files are
  **returned to families for free.**
- **How to search / what to send:** submit the web form with the ancestor's **maiden
  name** (all variants), DOB, birthplace, and known UK details; note that an older
  refugee may have arrived outside the Kindertransport age range, so ask them to
  check **any** refugee/arrival/case record 1938–40, not only Kindertransport. Only
  ~35k of ~65k refugees have a surviving case file, so a registration card alone may
  be all that exists.
- **Turnaround:** weeks (email response from the archives team). **Cost:** free.

## Other UK refugee archives — Wiener Holocaust Library & AJR

Two more UK sources worth a request if your ancestor resettled in Britain:

- **The Wiener Holocaust Library (London)** — https://wienerholocaustlibrary.org —
  the UK's oldest Holocaust archive, rich in **refugee documentation, family papers,
  eyewitness accounts, and the "enemy alien"/refugee context**. It ran the HO 396
  digitisation project (see Kew, above) and holds refugee family collections and
  survivor testimony that can corroborate the flight story. Search its online
  catalogue; contact the reading room / enquiries team for holdings on a named
  individual or family.
- **AJR — Association of Jewish Refugees** — https://www.ajr.org.uk — the
  membership body of Jewish refugees from Nazi Europe who settled in Britain. Its
  **Refugee Voices** testimony archive and membership/biographical records can
  document an ancestor as a recognised Nazi-era refugee and add biographical
  colour. Enquire via the AJR for any record or testimony naming your ancestor.

## If your ancestor fled ELSEWHERE — equivalent record types by destination

The UK set above is one worked example. Whatever the destination, look for the same
**four record categories**, and substitute the local institution:

1. **Immigration / naturalisation file** — the state record of the refugee's arrival
   and later citizenship (arrival manifests, alien-registration files, naturalisation
   petitions). Usually names birthplace, DOB, prior nationality, and arrival date —
   proving both flight and identity.
2. **Refugee-aid organisation records** — the Jewish/refugee relief body that
   assisted the arrival kept registration cards and case files (like the UK's CBF).
3. **National census / population registration** — a government headcount that places
   the person in the country at a date (like the UK's 1939 Register).
4. **Civil-registration certificates** — local birth/marriage/death certificates for
   the descent chain (like the UK's GRO).

**By destination:**

- **United States. Petition the right agency — the split is by date (27 Sept 1906).**
  A Nazi-era refugee who arrived and naturalised in the late 1930s–40s falls on the
  **post-1906** side, so:
  - **Post-27-Sept-1906 naturalisation records (C-files, and A-files) → the USCIS
    Genealogy Program**, *not* NARA. Since the 1906 Basic Naturalization Act, the
    federal Naturalization Service kept a duplicate of every naturalisation in a
    centralized **Certificate File (C-file)**; these, plus **Alien Files (A-files,**
    the rich immigration case file opened from ~1940 on**)**, are held by **USCIS**
    and obtained through its **fee-based Genealogy Program records request**
    (uscis.gov/records/genealogy — an index-search request then a records request).
    For a 1930s–40s Austrian-Jewish refugee, **this is usually where the immigration/
    naturalisation file actually lives.**
  - **Pre-27-Sept-1906 naturalisations, and many ship passenger manifests → NARA**
    (archives.gov; the National Archives Catalog) — many indexed/imaged on Ancestry,
    FamilySearch, and the Statue of Liberty–Ellis Island manifests. NARA also holds
    some other historical immigration records. (Older A-files eventually transfer to
    NARA regional archives, but the modern route for a WWII-era refugee is USCIS.)
  - Refugee-aid: **HIAS** and the **National Refugee Service / American Jewish Joint
    Distribution Committee ("the Joint")** case files. Census: US Federal Census
    (1940, 1950). Vital records: the relevant **state** vital-records office. USHMM
    (already core, Tier A) is US-based and especially rich here.
- **Palestine / Israel.** Immigration under the British Mandate (immigration
  certificates, the "Palestine" arrival records) and, post-1948, Israeli **State
  Archives** and the **Population Registry**. **Central Zionist Archives**
  (Jerusalem) holds immigration/aid records. Yad Vashem (Tier A) is Israel-based and
  central for this destination.
- **France.** Naturalisation and *étranger* (foreigner) files at the **Archives
  nationales** and departmental archives; internment/refugee records where relevant.
  Refugee-aid via **OSE** and HICEM. Civil registration (*état civil*) at the local
  *mairie* / departmental archives.
- **Shanghai.** The Shanghai ghetto refugee community — **HICEM** and the
  **Committee for Assistance of European Jewish Refugees in Shanghai (CFA)** records;
  emigration files at the **Shanghai Municipal Archives**; USHMM and Yad Vashem hold
  Shanghai refugee collections.
- **Argentina.** Immigration/arrival records via the **Centro de Estudios
  Migratorios Latinoamericanos (CEMLA)** and the **Archivo General de la Nación**;
  refugee-aid via local Jewish community bodies (e.g. **AMIA**) and HIAS-successor
  organisations. Civil registration via the *Registro Civil*.
- **Australia.** Migrant arrival records, alien-registration, and naturalisation
  files at the **National Archives of Australia** (naa.gov.au — RecordSearch,
  much imaged online). Refugee-aid via the **Australian Jewish Welfare Society**.
  Civil registration via the state Births, Deaths & Marriages registry.
- **Canada.** Immigration and naturalisation records via **Library and Archives
  Canada (LAC)** (bac-lac.gc.ca — immigration/border-entry records and the
  naturalisation index). Refugee-aid: **JIAS (Jewish Immigrant Aid Services /
  Jewish Immigrant Aid Society)** case and settlement records. Civil registration
  (birth/marriage/death) is **provincial** — order from the relevant province's
  vital-statistics office.
- **Bolivia / Brazil (and South America generally).** Often overlooked, but
  significant: **Bolivia** took in on the order of **~7,000–10,000 Austrian and
  German Jewish refugees** in the late 1930s (the Mauricio Hochschild / "Sociedad
  de Protección a los Inmigrantes Israelitas" rescue). Look for **immigration/
  arrival records in the national immigration archives**, Jewish-community
  (Círculo Israelita) records, and refugee-aid case files (HICEM / the Joint
  operated across South America). **Brazil**: arrival and *estrangeiro* registration
  records at the **Arquivo Nacional** and immigration-memorial archives, plus local
  Jewish-community records. In both, HICEM, the Joint, USHMM, and Yad Vashem (Tier A)
  hold South-America refugee collections — start the cross-check there.
- **Anywhere else.** Search the national archives for "aliens registration /
  naturalisation / immigration," find the local Jewish refugee-aid organisation of
  the 1930s–40s, and locate the national civil-registration authority. The **Arolsen
  Archives, USHMM, and Yad Vashem** (all Tier A) span every destination and are the
  best starting cross-check no matter where the ancestor went.

## A note on document format for the declaration

For the §58c **declaration** itself, ancestor and descent-chain documents are
generally accepted as **plain photocopies — no apostille, no certified copies, no
translation** where they're already in German or English. Typically only each
**applicant's own** birth certificate and criminal-record extract need apostilles.
(Confirm the exact list with your responsible consulate — this can vary and is
decided per case.) Civil-status documents (marriage/divorce) may be **unnecessary
for the declaration** but required later at the **passport** stage. This means the
archive hunt above is mostly about *finding* the records, not about expensive
certification.
