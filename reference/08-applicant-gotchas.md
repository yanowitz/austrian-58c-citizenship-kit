# §58c StbG — Applicant Gotchas (the hard-won realities)

The things that trip people up, drawn from official pages plus first-hand accounts in r/AustrianCitizenship and the "Austrian Citizenship Holocaust Descendants" Facebook group.

Source-tier legend: **[T1 / OFFICIAL]** = BMEIA / MA35 published · **[T2 / LAW]** = law-firm or Kultusgemeinde · **[T3 / ANECDOTE]** = single Reddit/Facebook poster (low tier, one data point) · **[INFERENCE]** = synthesis.

> ⚠️ **Staleness note.** §58c-for-descendants opened **1 Sept 2020**; the extended provision came into force **1 May 2022**. Early-cohort (2020–21) timelines are inflated by COVID + first-wave backlog and over-estimate current waits. Treat older data points accordingly.

---

## 1. The consulate is a mailbox — MA35 in Vienna decides [T3, cross-corroborated]

Multiple applicants report the **consular officer praised the file and signaled a likely win — then MA35 rejected.** One was told by a London officer it was *"one of the most comprehensive sets of documents he had ever received"* and was still denied. **Consular optimism ≠ outcome.** Build the file for MA35's adjudication, not for the officer's reaction.

---

## 2. MA35 does NOT do your historical research for you [T3, with lawyer corroboration]

The official pages say Austria will "try to find evidence in archives" — but in practice, for **complex historical constellations, MA35 does not independently reconstruct the story.** In a well-known DP-camp descent case the applicant submitted an Austrian birth cert, DP-camp roll, identity certificate with exit stamps, full genealogy, and a legal argument about MA35's duty to investigate on its own motion — **rejected, and the administrative court upheld it.** The applicant's lawyer's lesson:

> MA35 does not research complex cases for you; the applicant must reconstruct the entire documentary/historical context — and even then the evidence is discarded if it doesn't match the **standard civil-registry document types** the office expects, *even when those document types could not possibly have existed* in that historical context.

**Core lesson:** give MA35 exactly the **2–3 standard registry documents** it expects (birth / marriage / naturalization registry records), not a historically-clever substitute. The archive-search "relief" is real for straightforward cases but do not *rely* on it for anything non-standard.

---

## 3. MA35 searches sloppily — pre-empt it [T3]

One denial letter revealed MA35 had: (a) found "no record of the ancestor living in Austria" **despite her Austrian address being on a submitted official document**; (b) searched the **wrong city**; (c) searched under the ancestor's **married name instead of her maiden name**; and (d) **never addressed the specific legal sub-provision** the applicant actually applied under.

**Lesson:** state, explicitly and redundantly, the ancestor's **maiden name**, the **exact town**, and the **exact §58c sub-provision** you're invoking. Assume the caseworker will otherwise search wrong. A **family-tree diagram** in the file helps them follow the linkage [T3].

## 4. Non-Jewish / political / "fear of persecution" claims are much weaker [T3]

- *"Applications are very very rarely approved for people without Jewish descent"* — and multiple posters could not find a single non-Jewish approval.
- **Post-war departures fail on causation.** The NSDAP / German Reich dissolved in 1945; departures in **1948–1951** get argued as not connected to Nazi persecution, and "fear of persecution" as already non-existent. The statute requires persecution or fear "by organs of the NSDAP or the German Reich" — a hard sell for post-'45 flight.
- **Forced-labour / "total deployment" (Zwangsarbeiter) is contested** — there were judgments that total deployment was *not* persecution; an uncertain category.

**[INFERENCE]** The strong, routinely-approved profile is a **Jewish ancestor who fled Nazi persecution 1938–45**. If that's your anchor, the failure modes above don't apply to you — your risk is timeline and documentary completeness, not eligibility category.

## 5. What evidence MA35 accepts vs. rejects

- **Arolsen Archives records ALONE can be deemed "not sufficient"** [T3] — one pending case had to supplement after submitting only Arolsen records. Pull Arolsen as **supporting** evidence, not as your sole proof.
- **Victim recognition / National Fund (Nationalfonds) pension is very strong evidence** [T3]. An ancestor recognized by Austria as a persecution victim (e.g. receiving a National Fund victim's pension, or "registered as a refugee from Vienna") produced applications the consulate itself called solid. **If any victim-recognition or pension paper exists for your anchor, it is high-value — hunt for it.**
- **A historical registration record (Meldezettel / Meldekarte)** proving the ancestor's last Austrian address is often the single item you can't easily produce yourself. Leads that have worked [T3]: contacting **local Austrian authorities directly**; the **ICRC (Red Cross) tracing service** (obtained one applicant's grandmother's *Meldekarte* with her 1941–45 addresses); **findbuch.at** (for a Jewish ancestor there is often a large paper trail that *exists but isn't digitized* — register and search); and paying the Austrian archives to research the ancestor.
- **Creative documentary evidence can work** [T3] — a published book naming an ancestor as a University of Vienna graduate served as proof of prior citizenship (matriculation presupposed a birth certificate). Any published/printed reference naming your ancestor can bolster the file.
- **Nationality shown as "German" on 1938–45 records is NOT a discrepancy** [T1 handles routinely] — after the March 1938 Anschluss, Austrians were documented as German nationals. The §58c form correctly reflects Austrian origin/citizenship pre-annexation; MA35 handles this as a matter of course. Don't be alarmed by "German" on a wartime alien/refugee card.

## 6. Apostille & criminal-record mechanics — the active blocker

Applies wherever you live — only the specific offices change. Your **own** birth certificate and **own** criminal-record certificate are the two documents that need authentication; ancestor / descent-chain docs do **not**.

- **Own birth certificate → apostille (or, for non-Hague countries, consular legalisation) by your country's competent authority.** A copy in hand is "half good" — order a **fresh certified original** (see the process file, §7). *US example:* the apostille comes from the **issuing state's Secretary of State**.
- **The birth cert and the criminal-record cert may be authenticated by DIFFERENT offices** in the same country — don't assume one office handles both. *US example:* the FBI criminal-record report is a **federal** document, so its apostille (if needed) comes from the **US Dept of State Office of Authentications**, NOT a state office. [T3, cross-corroborated] Applicants elsewhere follow the same split-by-document-type logic with their own national authorities.
- **Whether the criminal-record certificate needs an apostille AT ALL can be disputed** — some applicants report submitting the US FBI report with no apostille and being fine; others were told to apostille it. **[ACTION]** Ask your consular handler *before* paying for/waiting on an apostille; you may save time and money.
- **Embassies sometimes wrongly demand a re-apostille** [T3] — at least one applicant was told to redo an apostille, which turned out to be an internal miscommunication ("your documents were verified, we don't need them apostilled again"). If asked to redo an apostille, **push back / confirm before paying twice.**
- **Every applicant's birth cert needs its own apostille** — including each child's. Ancestor / descent-chain docs do **not** need apostilles.

### Criminal-record validity window — sequence it carefully [T3, cross-corroborated]

The national criminal-record certificate has a validity window (for the **US FBI Identity History Summary**, commonly cited as **~6 months**; other countries' certificates have their own), and the clock is on **SUBMISSION**, not processing:

> "The expiration date on your background check is the deadline for you to submit it to your embassy/consulate." … "As long as the embassy sent your file to MA35 within the time limit, you're fine — everything is frozen at the date MA35 receives it; citizenship is backdated to that date."

**[ACTION]** Sequence the check so it's **inside its validity window when you actually file** — don't pull it early while waiting on the birth-cert/apostille track, or it expires and you re-pull. For a family filing together, pull everyone's certificates close together so the whole bundle is simultaneously valid at submission.

## 7. The loss-of-citizenship trap [T1 / OFFICIAL]

Acquiring **another** foreign citizenship **after filing** — even while your §58c case is still pending — causes **retroactive loss** of the Austrian citizenship being granted. **Do not naturalize into any new citizenship mid-process.**

- **Related, and NOT a red flag:** shortly after filing (~3 days), MA35 unit 8.2 typically emails a routine **"other citizenship / military service" letter** asking you to acknowledge that acquiring a *new* citizenship *after* the grant would cost you the Austrian one. This recurs across the applicant community and **does not signal a problem** — don't panic when it lands. It simply restates the trap above.
- **Military service — theoretical, essentially inapplicable abroad.** [T1 / INFERENCE] Austria has conscription, and a male citizen carries a *theoretical* liability for it. In practice it is **inapplicable while you reside abroad** — the obligation only becomes live if a male citizen **takes up residence in Austria** (and even then it is age-bounded). For a diaspora §58c beneficiary who never moves to Austria, the "military service" clause in the letter above is essentially a non-issue; it's mentioned for completeness, not as a real burden. Confirm specifics with your consulate if you plan to live in Austria.

## 8. Minor vs. adult children — separate declarations [T1 / OFFICIAL]

- **Each child needs a SEPARATE declaration.** Official: *"A separate declaration is required for each child."*
- **Minor children** are filed *by a parent on the child's behalf*, and can be bundled with the parent's filing (a minor filing may additionally require the parent's apostilled marriage cert + the other parent's birth cert).
- **Adult children are independent declarants** — each files their **own** declaration at **their own** consular district (jurisdiction = each person's main residence, and it's exclusive). This is why a family of adults can end up filing at **multiple consulates**.
- **Shared ancestor evidence** need only be submitted once across a family; the others reference the prior filing (see the process file, §3). When relatives apply off the same ancestor, give each consulate the related applicants' **names, DOBs, filing dates, and consular-officer contacts**, and ask the officers to attach the shared ancestor documentation / link the files. Attaching an already-approved relative's decision (*Bescheid*) helps.

## 9. Realistic timelines [T3 data + T2 estimates]

| Reported duration | Tier / context |
|---|---|
| **48 days** (application → notification) | [T3] fastest data point seen |
| **~4 months** | [T3] clean, well-documented case; MA35 proactively requested a missing marriage cert, then approved |
| **8 months** | [T3] **identical case, same embassy, same year** as the 4-month one — yet 2× as long, never contacted. Shows large variance unrelated to merits |
| **~15 months** | [T3] "complex legal question" / "prevented return" clause; also early-COVID cohort |
| **12–24 months** | [T3] community rule-of-thumb for a full decision |
| **"3–9 months"** | [T2] the *official* firm estimate — **materially rosier than lived experience** |

**[INFERENCE]** For a clean, well-documented case expect **~4–15 months; plan for 12–24.** The official "3–9 months" is optimistic. Variance is large and appears driven by MA35 internal load and whether a caseworker requests supplements — **partly a lottery independent of case merits.**

**Reassuring counter-base-rate [T3]:** an experienced paid facilitator reported assisting ~120 families / ~650 people over 3 years with ~70% already approved or in-process and **no "ugly battle"** in any — framing the rare hostile/denial stories as the exception, not MA35's norm, for the strong (Jewish-flight) profile.

## 10. Miscellaneous hard-won tips

- **Mailing your passport is fine and fast** [T3] — multiple applicants mailed passport + originals to their consulate and got them back in **~9 days** (USPS Priority / UPS 2-day). Personal appearance is generally **not** required; the consulate copies your originals and returns them fast.
- **§58c is *declaratory*** — you are a citizen from the **date MA35 *receives* the declaration** (frozen at receipt by the competent authority, not the approval date; because the consulate forwards it, that receipt date can be a bit later than the day you hand it in — confirm which date controls with your post). After approval you mail back the signed document MA35 sends.
- **Do NOT donate original family documents to a museum expecting to retrieve them** [T3] — at least two applicants donated to the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and the docs went "out for digitization" and became inaccessible for years. Keep your own certified copies.
- **Proactively include the parents' marriage certificate** [T3] — one applicant was fast-tracked only after MA35 asked for exactly this; a comparable applicant who wasn't asked waited twice as long. Front-loading standard descent-chain records may prevent a mid-process request.
- **Keep first names consistent across birth cert and marriage cert** [T3] — if they match, caseworkers usually won't probe out-of-line name changes; you generally only need marriage/divorce certs for links **in the direct descent line**.
